AI-Assisted Literature Curation

Workflow designer and evaluator | LLMs, Prompt Design, CIViC

Overview

Compared 4 LLM tools for drafting cancer-variant evidence statements and wrote the team's human-in-the-loop workflow and master prompt.

Curating clinical evidence from oncology papers is slow because every paper must be read closely. The question was whether large language models could draft evidence statements that a curator can trust, not just shorter summaries.

Tools

Role

Workflow designer and evaluator. Co-op project at Canada's Michael Smith Genome Sciences Centre (BC Cancer) (Sept 2025 – Apr 2026).

Description

  • Compared 4 tools on real curation tasks and identified which handled long, rule-based prompts and structured output reliably.
  • Wrote a master prompt that sets the curator role, the evidence standards, formatting rules, statement structures for each evidence type and worked examples.
  • Defined a 3-step workflow: initialize the session with the prompt and the curation standard, supply the paper, then verify the output.
  • Built a verification checklist around the most common failure, missing or misreported numbers: sample sizes, endpoints, hazard ratios, confidence intervals and p-values.
  • Found that larger batches mixed metrics between articles, and set a limit of 5 to 6 papers per session.
  • Documented the method as a wiki guide, including a recovery prompt for broken formatting and a rule never to include personal identifiers.

Outcome

The workflow reduces drafting effort while keeping a curator as the final check on every statement. I came away treating prompt engineering as an evaluation problem: the useful work was finding where outputs fail and designing the review that catches it.
