Spinal Cord Scaffold Design Proposal

Design contributor (ethics and regulatory analysis) | Literature Review, ISO Standards, Cost Modeling

Overview

A design proposal for a regenerative spinal implant, with the regulatory pathway mapped from in vitro testing to clinical trial application.

Spinal cord injuries leave a gap that axons do not regrow across. The team proposed a fibrin scaffold carrying carbon nanotubes and a patient's own stem cells to bridge it, and had to show a credible path to the clinic.

Tools

  • Literature review
  • ISO 10993-5
  • Health Canada CTA guidance
  • cost modelling

Role

Design contributor (ethics and regulatory analysis). UBC course project, team of three.

Description

  • Defined benchmark criteria for biocompatibility, mechanical durability and axon regrowth from preclinical literature and ISO 10993-5.
  • Mapped the approval pathway from in vitro validation to Health Canada's Clinical Trial Application and GMP-compliant manufacture.
  • Proposed a low-cost fabrication model using converted 3D printers for bioprinting.
  • Addressed consent and privacy with PIPA-aligned protocols.

Outcome

This is a paper design, not a built device. It was my introduction to how regulatory and ethical requirements shape an engineering design from the start.
