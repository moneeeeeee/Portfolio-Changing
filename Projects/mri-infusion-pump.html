MRI-Compatible Infusion Pump

Embedded systems developer and mechanical designer | SolidWorks, Arduino, MATLAB, 3D Printing

Overview

A low-cost, fully non-ferrous syringe pump with 3D-printed gear actuation and pressure feedback control, for use near MRI scanners.

Standard infusion pumps contain ferrous parts that pose safety and interference risks near an MRI scanner. The team set out to design a low-cost fluid delivery system with no ferrous components.

Tools

  • SolidWorks
  • 3D printing
  • Arduino
  • MPX5010 sensor
  • TinkerCAD
  • MATLAB
  • MS Project

Role

Embedded systems developer and mechanical designer. UBC biomedical engineering design project, team of five.

Description

  • Co-designed a gear-driven syringe driver made from 3D-printed PLA parts, converting stepper rotation to linear plunger travel through a gear-rail interface.
  • Integrated a differential pressure sensor with a Wheatstone bridge and instrumentation amplifier, and streamed readings to a computer in real time.
  • Co-developed closed-loop control that compares live pressure with a potentiometer-set target and steps the motor to hold it, with fault-handling logic.
  • Simulated the circuit in TinkerCAD before physical testing.
  • Planned build and test phases with a Gantt chart, coordinated procurement and kept requirements traceable.

Outcome

The project gave me hands-on experience with sensor integration and precision actuation, and showed me how design controls such as traceable requirements keep a five-person team aligned.
